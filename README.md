# Applications and Use Cases of Agentic AI

Agentic AI systems use AI agents to carry out tasks toward a defined goal. In multi-agent systems, agents have specialized roles and coordinate their work to complete complex business processes.

## 1. Insurance

AI agents can streamline insurance workflows such as:

- **Claims processing:** Review claim information and supporting documents.
- **Customer onboarding:** Collect and validate customer details.
- **Data processing:** Organize and analyze insurance data.

Specialized agents can handle different stages of a workflow and share their results.

**Business benefits:** Reduced administrative effort, improved decision accuracy, and more personalized customer service.

## 2. Marketing

Multi-agent systems can support marketing teams by dividing work among specialized agents:

| Agent | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Data collection agent | Gather customer data from sources such as social media |
| Sentiment analysis agent | Analyze customer opinions and feedback |
| Reporting agent | Generate reports to support marketing decisions |

The transcript highlights **CrewAI** as a framework for building collaborative AI agents.

**Business benefits:** Better-informed strategies, faster analysis, and more effective marketing efforts.

## 3. Retail

AI agents can personalize customer interactions across touchpoints such as:

- Email
- Chat
- Social media

Using customer relationship management (CRM) data and previous interactions, agents can tailor product recommendations and provide after-sales support.

**Business benefits:** Improved shopping experiences, stronger customer loyalty, and increased sales.

## 4. Healthcare

AI agents can support healthcare professionals through specialized tasks:

| Agent responsibility | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Medical image analysis | Help identify relevant findings in scans |
| Patient history evaluation | Review medical history and patient context |
| Diagnostic support | Assist clinicians in assessing possible conditions |
| Treatment recommendation support | Help clinicians evaluate treatment options |

**Potential benefits:** More efficient clinical workflows and better-informed care decisions.

Clinical decisions require appropriate professional oversight and validation.

## Key Takeaways

- **Specialization:** Each agent has a defined role and responsibility.
- **Collaboration:** Agents coordinate to complete a larger workflow.
- **Automation:** Systems can reduce repetitive administrative work.
- **Personalization:** Customer context can help tailor interactions and services.
- **Oversight:** Human review remains important for consequential decisions.

The value of agentic AI comes from coordinating tasks to achieve a measurable business outcome. Using multiple agents alone does not guarantee better accuracy or efficiency.

# Agentic AI in Healthcare: A Multi-Agent Scenario

## Overview

The lesson describes a healthcare provider, referred to by the pseudonym **Grey Sloan Medical Center**, that implemented a multi-agent AI system to improve diagnostics, patient scheduling, and administrative workflows.

## Business Challenges

The center faced several challenges:

- High diagnostic error rates
- Lengthy patient wait times
- Inefficient patient data management
- Heavy administrative workloads

The objective was to improve care quality while helping staff use their time and resources more effectively.

## Multi-Agent Solution

The system assigned specialized responsibilities to three groups of agents.

| Agent group | Responsibilities | Intended benefits |
|---|---|---|
| Diagnostic support agents | Analyze radiology images, lab results, and patient histories; suggest diagnoses and potential treatments | Support more accurate and timely clinical decisions |
| Patient management agents | Schedule appointments, match provider availability with patient needs, and adjust schedules using current data | Reduce wait times and improve provider utilization |
| Administrative agents | Update patient records, automate data entry, and process billing and claims | Reduce administrative effort and processing errors |

## 1. Diagnostic Support Agents

Diagnostic support agents analyzed multiple sources of clinical information:

- Radiology images
- Laboratory results
- Patient histories

They provided recommendations to physicians during the diagnostic process.

**Example:** An agent could identify a possible bone spur in an X-ray and suggest potential treatment options for a doctor to evaluate.

**Human oversight:** Physicians retained final authority over diagnosis and treatment.

## 2. Patient Management Agents

Patient management agents automated appointment scheduling by considering provider availability and patient needs.

They also used real-time data to adjust schedules and resource allocation.

### Reported Outcomes

According to the instructor, the deployment resulted in:

- Reduced patient wait times
- More timely consultations
- Fewer cancellations
- Better use of provider time
- An average clinic seeing **30% more patients**

The transcript does not specify the measurement period, baseline, or evaluation method for the 30% figure.

## 3. Administrative Agents

Administrative agents handled routine operational work, including:

- Entering data from multiple sources
- Keeping patient records current
- Processing billing
- Handling insurance claims

The goal was to reduce errors and administrative workloads, allowing healthcare staff to spend more time on patient care.

## Key Takeaways

- **Specialized roles:** Each agent group addressed a distinct operational or clinical need.
- **Coordinated workflows:** Multiple agents supported different parts of healthcare delivery.
- **Current data:** Scheduling decisions used up-to-date information.
- **Human accountability:** Doctors retained responsibility for clinical decisions.
- **Business value:** The scenario connected AI adoption to care quality, patient access, and staff capacity.
