# Applications and Use Cases of Agentic AI

Agentic AI systems use AI agents to carry out tasks toward a defined goal. In multi-agent systems, agents have specialized roles and coordinate their work to complete complex business processes.

## 1. Insurance

AI agents can streamline insurance workflows such as:

- **Claims processing:** Review claim information and supporting documents.
- **Customer onboarding:** Collect and validate customer details.
- **Data processing:** Organize and analyze insurance data.

Specialized agents can handle different stages of a workflow and share their results.

**Business benefits:** Reduced administrative effort, improved decision accuracy, and more personalized customer service.

## 2. Marketing

Multi-agent systems can support marketing teams by dividing work among specialized agents:

| Agent | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Data collection agent | Gather customer data from sources such as social media |
| Sentiment analysis agent | Analyze customer opinions and feedback |
| Reporting agent | Generate reports to support marketing decisions |

The transcript highlights **CrewAI** as a framework for building collaborative AI agents.

**Business benefits:** Better-informed strategies, faster analysis, and more effective marketing efforts.

## 3. Retail

AI agents can personalize customer interactions across touchpoints such as:

- Email
- Chat
- Social media

Using customer relationship management (CRM) data and previous interactions, agents can tailor product recommendations and provide after-sales support.

**Business benefits:** Improved shopping experiences, stronger customer loyalty, and increased sales.

## 4. Healthcare

AI agents can support healthcare professionals through specialized tasks:

| Agent responsibility | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Medical image analysis | Help identify relevant findings in scans |
| Patient history evaluation | Review medical history and patient context |
| Diagnostic support | Assist clinicians in assessing possible conditions |
| Treatment recommendation support | Help clinicians evaluate treatment options |

**Potential benefits:** More efficient clinical workflows and better-informed care decisions.

Clinical decisions require appropriate professional oversight and validation.

## Key Takeaways

- **Specialization:** Each agent has a defined role and responsibility.
- **Collaboration:** Agents coordinate to complete a larger workflow.
- **Automation:** Systems can reduce repetitive administrative work.
- **Personalization:** Customer context can help tailor interactions and services.
- **Oversight:** Human review remains important for consequential decisions.

The value of agentic AI comes from coordinating tasks to achieve a measurable business outcome. Using multiple agents alone does not guarantee better accuracy or efficiency.

# How Agentic AI Can Help Optimize Marketing Strategy

## Overview

As a marketing manager, you need to understand customers, plan campaigns, measure results, and adjust your strategy.

Agentic AI can support this work by coordinating specialized AI agents toward a shared marketing goal. Each agent handles a defined responsibility, uses available tools and data, and passes its findings to other agents or the marketing team.

For example, one agent might gather customer feedback, another analyze sentiment, and a third generate a report with recommended actions.

## 1. Gather and Organize Customer Data

A data collection agent can gather information from authorized sources such as:

- Customer relationship management (CRM) systems
- Social media interactions
- Website analytics
- Customer surveys and reviews
- Email campaign results
- Previous marketing campaigns

It can organize this information for analysis and flag missing or inconsistent data.

**Example:** An agent combines campaign engagement data with CRM records to help the team understand which customer segments are responding.

**Marketing value:** Less manual data preparation and a more consistent view of customer activity.

## 2. Analyze Customer Sentiment

A sentiment analysis agent can examine customer comments, reviews, and survey responses to identify:

- Positive and negative opinions
- Common complaints
- Frequently requested features
- Recurring questions
- Changes in customer perception

**Example:** Customers praise a product's features but repeatedly mention that its pricing is difficult to understand. The agent highlights pricing clarity as an issue for the marketing team to investigate.

**Marketing value:** Messaging can address concerns customers actually express.

**Limitation:** Sentiment analysis can misinterpret sarcasm, language differences, or context. Important findings should be checked against the original feedback.

## 3. Identify Customer Segments

A segmentation agent can group customers using relevant characteristics, such as:

| Segmentation approach | Example |
|---|---|
| Behavioral | Frequent website visitors who have not purchased |
| Engagement | Subscribers who regularly open campaign emails |
| Purchase history | First-time buyers and repeat customers |
| Expressed interests | Customers interested in a particular product category |

**Example:** The agent identifies an audience that frequently views product tutorials but rarely visits the purchase page. The team might test clearer product benefits or a stronger call to action for that audience.

**Marketing value:** Campaigns can be tailored to specific needs and behaviors.

## 4. Support Campaign Planning and Content Creation

A campaign planning agent can use approved customer insights to suggest:

- Campaign objectives
- Target audiences
- Messaging themes
- Content ideas
- Communication channels
- Campaign schedules
- A/B test hypotheses

A content agent can then draft material such as emails, social posts, and landing page copy.

**Example:** If customers frequently ask how a product works, the agents might propose an educational campaign and draft a series of tutorials.

**Marketing value:** Faster preparation of campaigns grounded in customer insights.

**Human responsibility:** The marketing team reviews brand voice, factual claims, creative quality, and campaign suitability before publication.

## 5. Monitor Campaign Performance

A performance analysis agent can track campaign metrics and flag changes that deserve attention.

| Metric | What it measures |
|---|---|
| Click-through rate (CTR) | The percentage of impressions that result in clicks |
| Conversion rate | The percentage of a defined audience or set of interactions that completes a desired action |
| Cost per acquisition (CPA) | Campaign cost divided by attributed acquisitions |
| Return on ad spend (ROAS) | Attributed revenue divided by advertising spend |
| Unsubscribe rate | The percentage of delivered email recipients who unsubscribe |

Metric definitions, attribution rules, and reporting periods should remain consistent when comparing results.

**Example:** An agent notices that an advertisement receives many clicks but few purchases. It flags possible issues with audience fit, the landing page, or the offer for investigation.

**Marketing value:** The team can identify problems earlier and focus its analysis.

## 6. Recommend Improvements and Experiments

An optimization agent can use campaign findings to propose changes, such as:

- Testing a different email subject line
- Improving a landing page call to action
- Adjusting messaging for a customer segment
- Reviewing an underperforming channel
- Testing a different content format

**Example:** An agent recommends testing two landing page headlines while keeping other campaign elements consistent.

The team should evaluate the results before deciding whether to adopt the change.

**Marketing value:** Campaign improvements become a structured learning process.

**Limitation:** A change in performance does not automatically prove what caused it. Seasonality, audience differences, tracking issues, and other factors may affect results.

## 7. Generate Reports for Decision-Making

A reporting agent can combine findings into a summary covering:

- Campaign performance
- Customer sentiment
- Segment-level results
- Emerging issues
- Recommended actions
- Questions requiring further investigation

**Example report:**

> Customers frequently ask about setup complexity. Tutorial content receives strong engagement, but purchases remain below the campaign target. The team should investigate the purchase journey and test messaging that explains the setup process.

**Marketing value:** Less time assembling reports and more time evaluating decisions.

## Example Multi-Agent Team

| Agent | Main responsibility | Typical output |
|---|---|---|
| Data collection agent | Gather and organize authorized marketing data | Prepared dataset |
| Sentiment analysis agent | Identify opinions and recurring feedback themes | Customer insight summary |
| Segmentation agent | Identify meaningful audience groups | Segment profiles |
| Campaign planning agent | Propose campaigns and experiments | Campaign brief |
| Content agent | Draft material using approved guidance | Content drafts |
| Performance analysis agent | Track results and investigate changes | Performance findings |
| Reporting agent | Consolidate findings and recommendations | Marketing report |

These are illustrative roles. A practical implementation may combine several responsibilities into one agent. More agents do not automatically produce better results.

## Example Workflow

1. **Define the goal:** Improve conversions for a product campaign.
2. **Collect data:** Gather campaign results, CRM information, and customer feedback.
3. **Analyze findings:** Identify customer concerns and audience patterns.
4. **Propose actions:** Suggest messaging changes and a measurable experiment.
5. **Review and approve:** The marketing manager checks the proposal and content.
6. **Execute within permissions:** Launch the approved campaign using authorized tools.
7. **Measure and learn:** Evaluate results and use the findings to guide the next campaign.

## What Makes This Agentic?

The agentic element is the system's ability to coordinate multiple steps toward a goal.

For example, a system may detect a drop in campaign performance, retrieve relevant data, analyze customer feedback, and prepare a proposed experiment for review.

Its autonomy depends on the permissions granted to it. An agent that prepares recommendations needs different controls from one allowed to publish content or change advertising budgets.

## Controls and Human Oversight

A marketing manager should establish clear boundaries:

- Use customer data only through authorized sources and for approved purposes.
- Review generated content for accuracy and brand consistency.
- Require approval for publishing, budget changes, and other consequential actions.
- Check data quality before relying on recommendations.
- Keep records of actions, approvals, and campaign changes.
- Evaluate results against a defined baseline and business goal.

## Potential Business Benefits

When implemented and evaluated effectively, agentic AI can help teams:

- Reduce repetitive research and reporting work
- Identify customer concerns sooner
- Prepare more relevant campaigns
- Respond faster to performance changes
- Run more structured experiments
- Spend more time on strategy and creative decisions

These are potential benefits, not guaranteed outcomes. Results depend on data quality, integration, agent reliability, and human oversight.

## Key Takeaway

Agentic AI can assist a marketing manager by connecting data collection, customer analysis, campaign planning, and performance reporting into a coordinated workflow.

The manager defines the goals, approves consequential actions, and evaluates whether the system improves marketing outcomes.
